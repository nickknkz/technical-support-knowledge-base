# Standard Operating Procedure: Ticket Handling

This workflow supports consistent intake, investigation, communication, escalation, and closure. Follow applicable local privacy, security, records, and service-management requirements.

## 1. Receive and acknowledge

- Create or locate one ticket for the issue; link duplicates rather than losing context.
- Capture requester and contact method, affected service/device, location if relevant, observed time, and a concise description.
- Acknowledge receipt through the normal channel. Do not promise a resolution time unless a defined target supports it.
- If there may be immediate safety, security, broad service, or data-integrity impact, use the relevant urgent escalation path.

## 2. Clarify impact and classify

- Ask what the user was trying to do, what happened, when it began, and whether the issue is reproducible.
- Record scope, business impact, affected function, workaround, and time sensitivity.
- Assign category, severity, and priority using the approved local model. Keep severity (impact) distinct from priority (response order).
- Note uncertainty and reassess when new evidence arrives.

## 3. Investigate safely

- Review known issues, service status, and relevant prior tickets or approved documentation.
- Gather only evidence needed to diagnose the issue. Record timestamps and exact error messages; redact credentials, tokens, and unnecessary personal information.
- Follow documented troubleshooting steps, changing one variable at a time when practical.
- Consider data loss, service disruption, and security implications before resets, reinstallation, permission changes, or repeated transactions.
- Record each action and result so another analyst can continue without repeating risky steps.

## 4. Resolve or escalate

- Apply a documented fix within your access and authorization. Follow change approval requirements for production or shared systems.
- If unresolved, escalate with a concise summary, impact, evidence, actions taken, and a specific request for help. State who owns the next action.
- Keep the requester informed of findings, workaround, risks, and the next update point in clear language.

## 5. Verify and close

- Confirm the original request or workflow works again; use an appropriate health check and requester confirmation when available.
- Record the resolution, relevant cause (confirmed or suspected), verification evidence, and any workaround or follow-up task.
- Close only when the issue is resolved or dispositioned under the local process. If waiting on the requester, document the contact and follow-up according to local rules.
- Link related incidents or problem records when they exist. Avoid asserting a root cause that has not been established.

## Ticket quality checklist

- [ ] Description is concise and understandable without private chat context.
- [ ] Impact and severity/priority rationale are recorded.
- [ ] Timeline, investigation, and outcomes are traceable.
- [ ] Sensitive information is excluded or stored only in an approved secure location.
- [ ] Escalation or resolution has a clear owner and next action.
- [ ] Recovery has been verified and communicated.
